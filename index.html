01 — The problem

The gap isn't technology.
It's everything around it.

AI is reshaping every industry. The conversation is still stuck inside developer circles — and the rooms that could fix that are built for spectators.

7/7

Problems we keep running into. One room we're building to fix them.

“Less hype. More shipped.”

The biggest AI impact won't happen in code editors. It will happen in hospitals, warehouses, banks, classrooms, and factories. But the people who understand those problems rarely sit with the people who can build solutions. Builders lack domain context. Domain experts lack builders. Both sides waste years not meeting.

The costYears of work, done twice, by people who never met.

Anyone can show an AI demo that works on stage. Far fewer know what breaks going from 10 users to 10,000, from a clean dataset to a messy enterprise one, from a prototype to something a company will trust. That knowledge lives with a small number of people, and it rarely gets shared.

The costEvery team learns the same expensive lessons alone.

Panels, keynotes, and networking mixers produce inspiration and business cards. They rarely produce collaboration, shipped work, or hires. People leave informed but not connected, and not any closer to building.

The costHigh attendance. Low outcome.

Companies hire on credentials and pedigree. Strong builders without the right brand name, college, or LinkedIn presence get overlooked. Meanwhile, companies searching for AI talent can't tell who has actually shipped.

The costThe best builders stay invisible.

Sponsoring a large open event buys you a logo on a banner and a crowd that never touches your product. The builders who would genuinely use your tools are hard to find and harder to reach in a meaningful setting.

The costReach, without relevance.

Some of the most capable builders are in India. Some of the most valuable problems and resources are in the US and Europe. The two ecosystems mostly run separately, and the collaboration that would help both rarely happens.

The costTwo ecosystems, one missed conversation.

Most communities run on goodwill until the organizers burn out. Without a path to paid work, the best people eventually leave. The network that helped them get started is the first thing they outgrow.

The costThe network leaks its best people.

What we do

We put the people who build AI in the same room as the people who live the problem.

noClue brings together engineers, founders, operators, and experts from healthcare, finance, logistics, education, manufacturing, and beyond. We put them in rooms with real problems, real tools, and each other. Then we help the best work — and the best people — get seen, backed, and paid.

Not a conference.
Not a Slack group.
A working network.

02 — The method

We don't host more events.
We build the system around them.

A room is only as good as what happens after it. These are the operating principles behind every noClue program.

01

Curate instead of open the doors.

Every room is selected. Smaller, sharper, and made of people who will actually build. Quality of conversation beats headcount.

02

Start from real problems, not trending topics.

Our programs are organized around how AI is used inside real enterprises — friction included. Not “the future of AI.” What's working, what's breaking, and why.

03

Show work, not slides.

Builders demo what they've made. Workshops build a real product across real tools. The format exists to produce output, not attendance.

04

Connect domains, not just developers.

We bring domain experts into the room because the best AI products come from people who deeply understand the problem. Engineers alone can't solve what they've never lived.

05

Make proof of work the signal.

Talent shows up through what it builds, demos, and ships — not whose name is on a degree. We help companies find builders by evidence.

06

Create paths to paid work.

Community is where we start, not where we stop. The long-term goal is a network where the people doing the work get paid for it.

07

Build bridges across borders.

We connect builders in India with communities in the US and Europe around shared problems, not just shared panels. The best rooms are the ones that don't already agree with each other.

05 — The signal

Proof of work
is the new résumé.

Companies can't tell who has shipped. Builders can't get seen without the right brand. We use the work itself as the signal.

We don't ask where you studied. We ask what you built, what broke, and what you'd do differently.

  • 01

    What you've shipped. A product, a demo, a teardown, a repo, an internal tool that saved your team a week.

  • 02

    How you think. The trade-offs you made, the parts that failed, and the instinct behind the next attempt.

  • 03

    Who you build with. Evidence you can work in a room — not just present on a stage.
